KYOTO HEART STUDY / VALSARTAN

What happens when trial data cannot be trusted?

The KYOTO HEART Study tested the blood-pressure drug valsartan. Its published findings were retracted in 2013 after the journal identified serious problems with the reported data.

+ High blood pressure Valsartan study
Why this matters for Probe

Drug studies need reliable records and conclusions. Probe tests those risks before enrollment, using simulated trials.

01 / TREATMENTValsartan + usual careCompared with usual care without valsartan
02 / PARTICIPANTS3,031 patientsPeople with uncontrolled high blood pressure and elevated cardiovascular risk
03 / PRIMARY OUTCOMEHeart and stroke eventsCardiovascular events, including fatal and nonfatal outcomes
04 / WHAT HAPPENEDRetracted in 2013The journal reported critical problems with the study data

01 / WHAT HAPPENS

A complete trial check.
Four simple steps.

Probe runs on a simulated drug study, so teams can spot problems before people participate.

01/

Choose the study scenario. Probe creates sample sites and patient records for a fictional diabetes drug. No real trial patient records are used.OPEN THIS STEP ↗
02/

Probe generates made-up records for an entire trial site and runs them through the fraud-monitoring checks. It measures which fake records get through.OPEN THIS STEP ↗
03/

Probe adds a monitoring check and tests it on different simulated sites. It also checks that genuine sites are not flagged more often.OPEN THIS STEP ↗
